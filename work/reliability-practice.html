04 · Reliability Engineering

Reliability as a Measurable Practice

Turning "we should be more reliable" into a budget teams can actually plan against.

  • 30+clusters under SLOs
  • 100+teams in reliability reviews
  • 3+SREs mentored

Problem

Reliability without numbers is an opinion, and opinions lose to feature deadlines. Teams knew incidents were bad but had no shared, quantitative basis for deciding when stability should outrank velocity — so the decision defaulted to velocity every time, until something broke badly enough to reset the argument.

Approach

Defined SLIs, SLOs, and error budgets grounded in EKS cluster health metrics and add-on component monitors across the fleet, then tied error budget consumption directly to feature prioritization — when the budget is spent, reliability work takes the slot, and that is a rule rather than a negotiation. Owned the incident lifecycle end to end: detection, response, communication, postmortem quality, and follow-up completion for high-severity customer-facing incidents. Ran reliability reviews for high-risk changes and new services covering production readiness, capacity, failure modes, and rollback — framed as teaching rather than gatekeeping, because a review that only blocks does not scale to 100+ teams. Mentored 3+ SREs and codified production readiness checklists and on-call standards built to outlive my involvement.

Impact

Repeat incidents declined through blameless postmortems that produced genuine follow-through rather than tickets nobody closes. Reliability became something teams could plan against instead of a value they were told to hold. The checklists and on-call standards persisted after I stepped back — the only real test of whether a practice took.
